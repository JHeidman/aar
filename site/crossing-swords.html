Scenario briefing: Scenario book p. 4

Victory: the Warsaw Pact must capture hex 0401 and hold it at the end of turn 5, and still control hex 0410. NATO wins by stopping either one. (The scenario card doesn't name a side; see open questions.)

What's switched off: no air, navy, weather, supply checks or new supply points. It's purely a ground fight.

Supply points (SP) still matter. In Crossing Swords they're a budget you spend on stronger combat formations. Once they're gone, they're gone.

2. Reading units and stacking

  • Each counter shows a combat strength and a movement allowance (bottom right). See the counter legend.
  • Size is measured in REs (regiment equivalents): a brigade or regiment is 1 RE, a division 3 RE, an HQ 1 RE, a battalion ½ RE. 1.2.3
  • Stacking limit: 6 RE per hex. Overstacking is only checked when a hex fights; the extra units then add nothing but still share any losses. 10.1
  • The one exception is Deliberate Assault, which lets 9 RE attack from a hex. 10.1

3. How a turn works

Every turn: decide initiative → assign modes → three Action Stages → end. Sequence of play chart 9

The initiative player (Warsaw Pact here) gets the first two Action Stages to attack. The reaction player mostly responds, and only attacks in the third. 9

The golden rule: each ground unit moves once per turn, at most. Combat only happens in the combat phase at the end of each Action Stage, never during movement. 9 Keep the Action Stage Summary open while you play.

4. Frontline and Reserve

At the start of each turn, every unit is either Frontline (no marker) or Reserve (Reserve marker). 15.2 Mode decides when a unit may move, per the table above.

  • Reserve needs an HQ. The unit must be in command range of an HQ from its own formation, and each HQ can hold only so many REs in reserve. 15.1.3 15.2.2
  • Attacking ends Reserve. The moment a Reserve unit declares an attack, it becomes Frontline. 15.2.2
  • Why bother? For the attacker, Reserve units are the second wave in Action Stage 2. For the defender, they're the units that can shift around during your opponent's stages and hit back in Action Stage 3.

5. Movement and zones of control

  • Pay the terrain cost for each hex entered, per the Terrain Effects Chart. Roads cost ½ MP. Unused MP are lost. 11.1
  • Zone of control (ZOC): a stack with at least 3 RE of assault units controls the six hexes around it. A lone brigade has no ZOC. 2.6
  • Entering an enemy ZOC: stop. Leaving one: +2 MP. If you start in enemy ZOC and haven't moved yet, you may go straight into another enemy ZOC hex, then stop. 2.6
  • Your own units in a hex don't cancel the enemy's ZOC there. 2.6

Repulse (overrun while moving)

A big unit can brush a small one aside during movement, at no MP cost, when it enters or starts next to an enemy. Compare printed strengths, with terrain. 11.1.2

  • 10:1 or better: automatic. 6:1 to 9:1: roll 1d10, succeeds on 1–5. Below 6:1: not allowed.
  • If it works, the defender retreats 1 hex and you keep moving (one repulse per move). If it fails, you stop.
  • Repulse isn't combat, so it doesn't count as a battle.

6. Ground combat, step by step

The attacker declares one combat at a time and resolves it before declaring the next. Each defending hex can be attacked once per combat phase; each unit fights once per phase. Attackers can come from several hexes; the defender is always one hex. 15.5 Everything below is on the ground combat chart.

  1. Declare. The attacker must be adjacent and able to cross the hexside. 15.5.1
  2. Tactical withdrawal (defender's choice). Roll 1d10 on the withdrawal table. Armor escaping infantry succeeds on 1–9; infantry escaping armor only on a 1. If it works, the defender slips up to 2 hexes away and the attacker must move into the empty hex. 15.5.2.1
  3. Combat formations, picked secretly and revealed together. They cost SP per unit, which is where your supply budget goes. 15.3 15.5.2.2
    FormationSP / unitEffect
    AC: Advance to Contact0Printed strength (the default)
    HA: Hasty Assault2Attack strength ×2
    DA: Deliberate Assault4Attack strength ×3, 9 RE stacking. Needs an HQ in range.
    HD: Hasty Defense21 column shift in the defender's favour
    DD: Deliberate Defense42 column shifts. Needs an HQ in range. Can't retreat: takes 1 extra hit instead.
    An HQ with artillery in range adds a bonus on top (attacker +1/+2/+3 to the die, defender 1 more shift). Stacks mixing formations, or units out of HQ range, can't use the Deliberate formations. 15.3
  4. Work out the odds. Total attack strength ÷ defense strength, rounded: 15 vs 6 is 2.5, which rounds to 3:1. Find that column on the odds table. 15.5.4
  5. Shift columns. Terrain, defensive formation, artillery and Battle-hardened all shift the column. The final column gives a die modifier, from −6 at 1:4 up to +9 at 10:1. 1:1 is ±0. 15.5.4
  6. Roll 1d10, add the modifier, and read the Combat Results Table. Results are hits for each side, sometimes with an R (retreat). If 5+ or 9+ RE fought on a side, that side may take extra hits. 15.5.5
  7. Apply results, defender first. See the next section.
  8. Clean up. Remove combat markers. Reserve units involved go to Frontline. Winners become Battle-hardened. 15.5.7

7. Hits, retreats and advancing

  • Hits: the first hit flips a unit to its reduced side, for good. Later hits use Hit markers, and each one lowers its ratings by 1. A unit at strength 1 that takes another hit is destroyed. 14.1 14.1.1
  • Retreat (R): every unit on that side moves back 1 hex, avoiding enemy ZOC (1 hit per ZOC hex if there's no other way). Defenders can't end next to the attackers. 15.6.1
  • Trading hits for ground: a defender may retreat up to 2 hexes instead of taking hits, 1 fewer hit per hex. A unit may also take 1 hit instead of retreating. 15.6.1 14.1
  • Advance after combat: if the defender's hex is empty, the attacker may move in, or follow along the retreat path, ignoring ZOC. An attacker who chooses not to advance may cancel 1 of its own hits. A retreating attacker never advances. 15.6.1

8. Initiative and Battle-hardened

  • Battles won: whoever takes more hits loses the battle; hits turned into retreats still count; ties don't count. Track it on the Battles Won marker. 15.5.6
  • Initiative: normally, whoever is ahead on Battles Won takes the initiative next turn; at 0 it stays put. 6.2 (In Crossing Swords, it may simply stay with the Warsaw Pact; see open questions.)
  • Battle-hardened (BH): a stack that wins a battle gets a BH marker, worth a 1-column shift in its favour. You can give BH up to cancel a hit. It's lost as soon as any unit joins or leaves the stack. 15.4.1

9. Tips and common mistakes

  • Budget your SP. The Warsaw Pact's 12 SP is roughly six units' worth of Hasty Assault for the whole game. Save it for the attacks that matter.
  • NATO's 40 SP buys defense. Hasty or Deliberate Defense on the two key hexes is where it pays off.
  • Small stacks don't block. Under 3 RE, a stack has no ZOC and the enemy walks past it.
  • Deliberate Defense means no retreat. Great for holding a victory hex, but the defender soaks up an extra hit when an R comes up.
  • Plan Reserve at the start of the turn. You can't change modes mid-turn, and Reserve is limited by your HQs.
  • Tactical withdrawal is a real option, especially for armor facing infantry. Giving up a hex can save a unit.

10. Open questions

The scenario card leaves two things unclear. These are our current readings; check the BoardGameGeek forums, where the designer answers questions.

  • Does initiative ever change? Probably not: the scenario's variant adds "determine initiative each turn with Battles Won", which suggests the base game keeps the Warsaw Pact as initiative player for all 5 turns.
  • Whose victory conditions are they? Most likely the Warsaw Pact's, since the variant gives NATO the mirror-image task.
