## Suggested path for us
1. **Crossing Swords** — twice, swapping sides. Then try the variant (woodland map, NATO attacks, initiative changes with battles won).
2. **Red Flag**, then **Sedan 1989**, to add air and combined arms.
3. Naval exercises (5–8, 10) if we're interested in the sea war; otherwise skip to Special Forces (9).
4. **B1 Fulda Gap** — the smallest battle scenario (3 GTs) — as the first "real" game.

## Crossing Swords primer

**Map:** Exercise map E1 · **Length:** 5 GTs · **Units:** NATO 10 ground, WP 7 ground · **Setup:** setup displays in the scenario book / Vassal.

**Victory (initiative side, WP):** capture **hex 0401** and hold it at the end of GT 5, **and** keep control of **hex 0410** at the end of GT 5. NATO wins by stopping either.

**Starting markers:** GT 1 of 5 · Battles Won 0 · Initiative: **WP** · Supply points: **NATO 40, WP 12**.

**Special rules:** no Supply Phase, no supply checks, no new SPs. Air, naval, DEFCON, weather and world opinion aren't used.

**Turn sequence used:** Initiative Determination → Unit Assignment → 1st, 2nd, 3rd Action Stage → End Stage.

**Things to watch:**
- **SPs are the scarce resource.** Hasty formations cost 2 SP per unit, deliberate ones 4. WP has only 12 SP for 5 turns, which is about six units' worth of Hasty Assault in the whole game. Plan when to spend them.
- **NATO has 40 SP and more units** but has to react. Hasty/Deliberate Defense on the key hexes is where those points go.
- **ZOCs need 3 RE.** A lone brigade doesn't stop anyone; a division or a 3-RE stack does.
- **Reserve mode** is how WP gets a second wave in Action Stage 2, and how NATO counterattacks in Action Stage 3.
- **Tactical Withdrawal** lets NATO trade ground for time instead of taking hits.

**Variant (later):** woodland map E2, **NATO** has the initiative and must capture 0410 / hold 0401, WP gets 2 RE of combat transport, and initiative is re-checked each turn with Battles Won.
